# SAM 2 / SAM 3 — where they fit, where they don't

**Status:** decided · **Date:** 2026-09-10

Short version: neither SAM model is a baseline we compete against. SAM 2 is a
label-making helper. SAM 3 is, at best, the same, and only for solar.

---

## Why not useful (as a baseline)

SAM 3 is an open-vocabulary "point at anything and name it" model, trained on
eye-level internet photos. Our images are straight-down satellite tiles of
Jaipur at ~26 cm/px. That is far outside what it has seen, so on a dense block
of flat grey concrete roofs it does one of two things:

- calls **everything** grey a building (roofs, lanes, empty plots all look the
  same from above), or
- decides there is **no rooftop here** and returns a blank mask.

Either way the number is bad for a boring, already-known reason: the model is
being used out of distribution. If our small model then beats it, a reviewer
just shrugs — "you used SAM 3 wrong." That makes it a weak comparison, not a
strong one. On top of that:

- The draft SAM 3 code was written against a **guessed API** (copied from a
  different tool).
- Running SAM 3 on the Google satellite tiles hits the **same licensing block**
  as everything else in the project — Google's terms forbid evaluating models
  on their map imagery.

SAM 3 also has **no domain-adaptation mechanism**, which is the whole point of
our pipeline. It cannot be the thing we improve on.

## Where useful (as a label helper)

**For the solar-panel stage**, where we have zero Indian examples:

- Prompt SAM 2 (or SAM 3) with "solar panel" / "solar water heater" across the
  Jaipur tiles.
- It returns a pile of candidate locations — many wrong, that is fine.
- Click through a few hundred: yes / no / actually a water tank.
- Result: a starter training set **plus** a set of hard negatives
  ("looks like a panel, isn't") we did not have before.

Its output is a time-saver for the tedious part. It **never appears in a
results table**.

For **rooftops** we already planned to use **SAM 2** for this same
label-assist role — it is smaller and good enough.

## What we verify against instead

Not SAM, not OSM. The real answer key is a **small hand-labelled sealed test
set** (~400 tiles, ~140 held out until the end), scored identically for every
pipeline. Supported by: Open Buildings as a city-wide cross-check, the
AIRS→Jaipur IoU drop and how much each step closes it, published figures from
prior Indian rooftop-solar papers, and a reality check against PM Surya Ghar
installed-capacity numbers for Rajasthan. OSM gets one line — "in the
well-mapped walled city our hand-drawn masks agree with OSM."
