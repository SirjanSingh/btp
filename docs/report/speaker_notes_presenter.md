# Presenter notes: Datasets to What worked (student-as-final version)

Spoken language first, then "Key numbers" for the technical detail. Results slides are presented by someone else.

## Slide 9: Datasets, source vs target
We use two datasets. On the left is AIRS from Christchurch, New Zealand: 857 training and 94 validation images at 7.5 cm per pixel, each with full roof masks. We trained our Round 1 U-Net on it. On the right is Jaipur: 16 big tiles, about 175 square kilometres, at 26.6 cm per pixel, which is 3.5 times coarser. For Jaipur we have no training labels at all. To test honestly, we hand-drew roofs on 152 crops of 512 by 512 pixels, split by tile into 76 calibration and 76 test crops, so no tile is in both halves. The hand crops are used only to choose a threshold and to score. They are never used to train.
Key numbers: AIRS 7.5 cm/px, 857 + 94 images; Jaipur 26.6 cm/px, 16 tiles, ~175 km²; 152 hand crops (512 x 512), 76 calib / 76 test.
Check before presenting: the slide says 187 crops reviewed; say 152 are scored unless the team has re-scored.

## Slide 10: Method overview
This is the whole method. First, our Round 1 AIRS model on Jaipur scores only 0.154 IoU, so it cannot be used as is. Instead we use CrossEarth, a remote-sensing foundation model already trained on the WHU building dataset, as a teacher. We run it on 8,920 Jaipur tiles that have no labels, and save its roof probability for every pixel. These are soft maps. Then we train a small ResNet-34 U-Net student on those soft maps. The student is not random: it starts from our AIRS weights, so what it learned on New Zealand roofs is adapted to Jaipur using only the teacher's maps. This is knowledge distillation. The student scores 0.706 IoU over three seeds, and we use it to measure roof area over the city and turn that into energy. No Jaipur label is used for training anywhere.
Key numbers: teacher zero-shot 0.677; student 0.706 ± 0.005 (3 seeds); 8,920 unlabelled tiles; city run 168 km².
If asked: we also self-trained CrossEarth itself and got 0.717, one run, inside the margin of error and much heavier, so it is not our final model.

## Slide 11: What is CrossEarth (hidden, use if asked)
CrossEarth is a published model by Gong and co-authors, arXiv 2410.22629, built to work on new regions without retraining. It has three parts. The backbone is DINOv2-L, a large vision transformer. Small Reins adapters sit inside the backbone and let it adapt to remote-sensing images without changing the big weights. On top is a Mask2Former head, which turns the features into masks. The authors trained it on the WHU building dataset; we use their released weights exactly as they are. We never train it on Jaipur. It has two classes, building and background, takes 512 by 512 tiles, and outputs a probability for every pixel.
Key numbers: DINOv2-L backbone, Reins adapters, Mask2Former head, 2 classes, WHU-Building A2S (aerial to satellite) weights; S2A variant exists.

## Slide 12: Teacher, soft map, student
This slide shows one real tile going through the pipeline. Step one, the teacher. CrossEarth runs on the tile with test-time augmentation, meaning we run it on flipped and rotated copies and average the results. The output is a roof probability from 0 to 1 for each pixel, saved as an image. We keep it as it is, with no thresholds. Step two, the student. It is a U-Net with a ResNet-34 encoder, initialised from our AIRS weights. Its target is the teacher's probability itself, not a hard yes or no. The loss is half soft binary cross-entropy and half soft Dice. We train with AdamW at learning rate 1e-4 and a one-cycle schedule with 10 percent warm-up, batch size 16, mixed precision, for 6 epochs. With 8,920 tiles that is 557 steps per epoch, about 3,300 steps in total. Augmentation is flips, 90-degree rotations and small colour changes. We report the last epoch, fixed in advance, never the best on test.
Key numbers: U-Net ResNet-34, 1 output channel; soft BCE + soft Dice (0.5 each); AdamW lr 1e-4, weight decay 1e-4; OneCycle, 10% warm-up; batch 16; 6 epochs, ~3,342 steps; 3 seeds.

## Slide 13: Why the teacher's scores need care
CrossEarth's scores are not normal probabilities. Its head already outputs values between 0 and 1, and then a softmax is applied again, so everything is squeezed into a narrow band: background sits at about 0.27 and the most confident roof at about 0.73. If we used a usual cut-off like 0.5 blindly, or assumed values near 0 and 1, we would get wrong labels. Our first attempt with cut-offs of 0.25 and 0.55 marked 81 percent of pixels as uncertain. So we never assume: the student's decision threshold, about 0.55, is chosen on the calibration crops only. The student learns from the soft values directly, so it needs no hard cut-offs. We did use hard cut-offs, roof at or above 0.50 and background at or below 0.30 with the middle ignored, when we also self-trained CrossEarth itself. Those were checked on the calibration crops and were 82 percent and 98.6 percent precise.
Key numbers: score range 0.27 to 0.73 (1/(1+e) = 0.269); student threshold ~0.55 from calib; self-training cut-offs 0.50 / 0.30, 82% and 98.6% precise, 16% of pixels ignored.

## Slide 14: From pixels to energy
Once we have a roof map, the rest is arithmetic. Each pixel is 0.266 by 0.266 metres, so area is pixel count times that squared. We could not measure how much of each roof is usable, because mapping tanks, stairwells and clutter on Indian roofs at this resolution is unreliable. So we assume 50 percent and show a range from 30 to 60 percent. Capacity uses India's MNRE guidance of about 10 square metres of shadow-free roof per kilowatt-peak. Energy is capacity times Jaipur's PVOUT from the Global Solar Atlas, 1,627 to 1,641 kilowatt-hours per kWp per year, times 0.95 for rooftop losses. We do not multiply by a performance ratio as well, because PVOUT already includes those losses and that would underestimate by about 22 percent. Panels already on roofs, found by our panel model, are subtracted. A worked example: 1,000 roof pixels is about 71 square metres, 35 usable, 3.5 kWp, and about 5,500 kWh a year.
Key numbers: GSD 0.266 m; k_usable 0.50 (0.30 to 0.60); 10 m² per kWp; PVOUT 1,627-1,641 kWh/kWp/yr; derate 0.95; existing PV 59-94 MWp subtracted.

## Slide 15: Experiments, test protocol
Before this round every Jaipur score compared the model with Open Buildings footprints, which were also the training labels, so it measured agreement, not accuracy. We fixed that with 152 hand-drawn crops covering all 16 tiles, drawn by 3 annotators. We split them by tile, 76 calibration and 76 test, so no tile appears in both. The calibration half is used only to choose the threshold. The test half is used only to report IoU, with a bootstrap confidence interval of about plus or minus 0.03. Our headline models never train on these crops. A few older baselines were scored on all 152 crops at a fixed 0.5 threshold, and we always mark those separately.
Key numbers: 152 crops, 3 annotators, 76 / 76 split by tile, IoU, 95% bootstrap CI about ±0.03.

## Slide 16: What failed
First, what did not work. Our AIRS model alone gives 0.154. Every method that adapts from the AIRS model stays below 0.32: about ten self-training variants and our own run of DUDA, a 2026 domain-adaptation method, at 0.274. Changing image colours with FDA or histogram matching was worse than doing nothing. A big part of the problem is calibration: the AIRS model predicts about 5.7 percent roof where the true share is about 28. We also tried training on Google's Open Buildings footprints after shifting them 7 pixels south and 3 west. That reaches 0.706 in Jaipur, but it needs a footprint database for the region, the footprints match real roofs at only 0.50 IoU, and the shift was tuned on hand labels. So it worked here but is not a portable method. Refining the teacher with SAM, the Segment Anything model, was worse in all 12 settings we tried. These baselines are our own implementations, not the authors' numbers.
Key numbers: AIRS 0.154; arms 0.04-0.32; DUDA 0.274; footprint route 0.706 (not portable, footprints 0.50 vs real roofs); SAM refinement 0.599-0.687 vs 0.701 plain.

## Slide 17: What worked
Two routes worked, and both use only the CrossEarth foundation model and unlabelled Jaipur tiles. Route 1, our final model, is the ResNet-34 student at 0.706 plus or minus 0.005 over three seeds. It beats its own teacher, 0.677, because it learns from the teacher's soft maps over thousands of tiles. Route 2 is self-training CrossEarth itself: we update only the adapters and the head, keep the DINOv2 backbone frozen, and train for 6,000 iterations with batch size 2, AdamW at 2e-5, and a linear warm-up then polynomial decay, about 65 minutes on one V100. It reaches 0.717 but from one run, and it is heavy. The two are statistically about equal, so we choose the small, stable student. Test-time augmentation over 8 flips and rotations adds about 0.01. Things that did not help beyond noise: seed ensembles, a bigger MiT-B2 encoder, an edge-weighted loss, longer training, and fine-tuning on 76 hand crops. A DINOv3 encoder was slightly worse.
Key numbers: student 0.706 ± 0.005; self-trained CrossEarth 0.717 (one run); 6,000 iterations, batch 2, AdamW lr 2e-5, weight decay 0.05, 200-iteration warm-up, resize 0.75-1.5 + 512 crop + flip + colour jitter; TTA +0.010.
