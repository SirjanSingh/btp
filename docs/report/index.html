Finding every rooftop in Jaipur without labelling one

A segmentation model trained only on Google Open Buildings footprints finds roofs in 26.6 cm satellite imagery of Jaipur, and the roof area feeds a city-scale rooftop-solar estimate. This report covers the full pipeline, every technique and the paper it comes from, what ran overnight, and what could be published.

Summary

The method is weak supervision, not unsupervised domain adaptation. We train a U-Net on Google's Open Buildings polygons over Jaipur, after moving them 7 px south and 3 px west to sit on the roofs. Seven domain-adaptation methods that start from a model trained on another city (AIRS, Christchurch) scored 0.04 to 0.33 IoU on the same hand-labelled test crops. The weakly supervised model scores 0.697, and 0.706 with test-time augmentation.

Overnight we tried five changes in parallel on the DGX (round 1) and then a teacher self-training hybrid (round 2). Best result, chosen on the calibration half: an ensemble of the shift+SAM-label model and the self-trained student, with TTA, at 0.712 test IoU (+0.015, 95% interval +0.009 to +0.022, over the plain model; +0.006 over the plain model with TTA). It reproduces with a second training seed (0.712 again). The honest summary: shift+SAM labels give a +0.008 single-pass gain that reproduces across two seeds, the self-trained student matches the TTA teacher in one pass, the edge-weighted loss and DINOv3 encoder did not help, and nothing stacked past about 0.71. 91% of the remaining error is partial boundaries (ignoring a 2 px band around roof edges gives 0.765), so the next step up is sharper roof outlines, not more weak-label tricks.

How to read the numbers. Every IoU here is measured against 152 hand-drawn roof crops split by map tile into a calibration half and a test half (76 crops each). Any setting (threshold, label variant, fusion weight) is picked on calibration; the number we quote is the test half. Gains come with a 95% paired-bootstrap interval. A change counts only if that interval stays above zero. Seed-to-seed noise is about 0.004–0.015.

Stage 1 · Data in
  • 16 Google satellite tiles of Jaipur, 26.6 cm per pixel (not the 10 cm first assumed), 175 km².
  • Cut into 512 × 512 crops with stride 512. Crops that are all black (outside the imagery) are skipped. Train: 7,371 crops from 13 tiles. Validation: 1,701 crops from tiles 1-1, 2-2 and 4-3.
  • Open Buildings v3 [3]: 523,283 polygons. Jaipur's true building share is 28.2% of pixels, against 7.7% in the AIRS training city: buildings here are also 23× smaller in pixels (median 913 px vs 21,084 px). That gap is why models from other cities fail.
  • scripts/make_instance_labels.py burns every polygon with its own ID, so touching buildings (78% of Jaipur buildings touch a neighbour) stay separable for the per-building steps.
Stage 2 · Labels, from footprint to roof
  • Rasterise, all confidences. Open Buildings confidence is mostly a size filter: keeping only conf ≥ 0.75 drops 89% of small buildings and 7% of large ones. On hand labels, training on all polygons wins (0.632 vs 0.605).
  • Global shift, 7 px south and 3 px west. Measured as the median best-overlap offset between Open Buildings and the hand roofs; the calibration half alone gives the same 7 / −3, so the test half does not leak. Worth +0.038 test IoU (CI +0.031 to +0.045). Idea from Girard et al. [6], in its simplest global form.
  • Per-building alignment. Each polygon is moved separately within ±14 px to the position that best overlaps the current model's TTA prediction (cv2.matchTemplate, ties go to the global shift; buildings under 150 px keep the global shift). The idea is from OMAF [14] and Align and Segment [15], done offline.
  • SAM refinement. Each polygon's box, padded 10 px, prompts SAM ViT-H [9] (one mask, batches of 64). SAM's outline replaces the footprint only if it overlaps it at IoU ≥ 0.3 and its area is 0.5–2× the footprint's; otherwise the footprint stays. About 70% of buildings are accepted. Pad and threshold were picked on the calibration half.
  • Teacher fusion (new, round 2). Soft label = w · teacher probability + (1 − w) · refined label. Cut at 0.5, this means the footprint lowers the teacher's bar inside a building and raises it outside: at w = 0.7 a pixel inside a footprint counts as roof above p = 0.29, outside only above p = 0.71. Trained with the soft values directly (--soft_masks), as Sirko et al. [3] did with soft teacher labels.

Stage 4 · Inference and the solar-panel detector
  • 8-way dihedral test-time augmentation: the crop is rotated 0/90/180/270° with and without a flip, each prediction is mapped back and the 8 probabilities are averaged. Worth +0.010 (CI +0.007 to +0.013). Averaging different models or seeds adds nothing: they make the same mistakes, which points at the labels.
  • Threshold 0.40, chosen on calibration. All models are under-confident on real roofs, so 0.3–0.4 beats the default 0.5.
  • Existing rooftop PV: a separate U-Net trained on the French BDAPPV panel set plus about 15,000 no-panel crops and 3,955 Jaipur ground negatives. It finds 0.444 km² of panels (about 56–89 MWp), a lower bound because no Jaipur panel labels exist.
Stage 5 · Energy chain

P (GWp) = A_roof × k_usable × η × 1 kW/m² with k_usable 0.60, η 0.20
E (TWh/yr) = P × PVOUT × derate with PVOUT 1,630 kWh/kWp/yr (Global Solar Atlas, 5 points: 1,627–1,641), derate 0.95

The roof area is divided by the model's measured over-prediction on the hand crops (1.21 on calibration, 1.07 on test) before it enters the formula. We never multiply PVOUT by a performance ratio: PVOUT already contains most system losses, and doing both under-estimates by 22.5% (diagnostic D15).

Evaluation protocol
  • 152 hand-drawn roof crops from three annotators, split by map tile into calibration (76) and test (76) halves with a similar annotator mix.
  • Pooled pixel IoU over all crops in a half. The label gate also ignores a 24 px border, where shifted labels are cut off.
  • Paired bootstrap over crops, 2,000 resamples, for every difference against the baseline.
  • Seed noise: 0.004 (three seeds of the shifted ResNet34) to 0.015 (older set). A single-seed gain below about 0.015 needs a second seed before we claim it.

How we got here: September to November

The project started as domain adaptation: take a rooftop model trained on AIRS (Christchurch, New Zealand) and adapt it to Jaipur without Jaipur labels. That failed every time. Open Buildings footprints, used directly as noisy labels, worked from the first try. The hand labels that arrived in November then reordered the September conclusions.

Where the remaining error lives

Overnight experiments

The error budget says the labels, not the model, are the limit. So round 1 changed one thing at a time against the baseline (ResNet34 on shifted labels, seed 42, 40 epochs): two model-side changes and three label-side changes. All five trained in parallel on the DGX V100s. Big files (SAM weights, extra libraries, refined labels, checkpoints) went to host /tmp because the home quota had 2 GB left.

Each candidate label set was scored directly against the hand roofs. This is cheap (minutes, no training) and tells us which label sets are worth a 2-hour training run.

Step 3: round 2, teacher self-training

The gate showed the trained model's own predictions (0.723 on test) are better labels than any refined footprint (best 0.650). Self-training on Jaipur failed in September, but those arms used hard labels from an out-of-domain AIRS teacher. Here the teacher is the in-domain 0.706 model and the labels stay soft, as in Sirko et al. [3], and each round is rebuilt from the original footprints, as Girard et al. [6] recommend.

What it means. The student learns the teacher's averaged-over-8-views answer and gives it back in one forward pass: 0.706 single-pass versus the teacher's 0.697, which makes it 8× cheaper to run for the full city. It does not beat the teacher with TTA by much (0.708 vs 0.706, within seed noise), so a third self-training round is not worth the GPU time. Fusing in the SAM footprint helps slightly (+0.002), which the gate predicted: on calibration, fusion w = 0.8 and the teacher alone were tied (0.688 vs 0.689).

Second seeds (seed 43). Shift+SAM labels: 0.705 and 0.706 single-pass for the two seeds, against 0.697 for the plain model, so a +0.008 single-pass gain that reproduces, and calibration agrees (0.677 and 0.673 vs 0.660). With TTA the two seeds give 0.704 and 0.708, the same as the baseline with TTA (0.706): the cleaner labels and TTA fix the same errors, so they do not stack. SAM-only labels did not reproduce (seed 43: 0.699 single-pass, 0.704 with TTA).

Best combination. Out of 510 single models and ensembles, the one with the best calibration score is the average of the shift+SAM model and the self-trained student, with TTA: 0.712 on test, +0.015 (+0.009 to +0.022) over the plain model, +0.006 over the baseline with TTA. Every top-10 calibration configuration lands between 0.706 and 0.713 on test. The overnight runs found a ceiling near 0.71, not a jump. The headline reproduces: swapping in the seed-43 shift+SAM model gives 0.712 again (+0.015, +0.010 to +0.021).

The modern techniques, in plain words

Weak supervision from open footprints

Instead of hand-drawing thousands of roofs, train on polygons someone else's detector already drew. The labels are noisy (offset, footprint not roof, missing buildings), but there are half a million of them in the right city. Kaiser et al. [4] showed this works with OpenStreetMap; Open Buildings [3] gives Jaipur coverage.

the core of the result

Label offset correction

Satellite images are not perfectly vertical, so a roof appears shifted from its footprint. Girard et al. [6] learn the shift; OMAF [14] (CVPR 2026) learns it per building. We use one global shift plus a per-building template match.

+0.038 IoU

Segment Anything (SAM)

A foundation model that cuts out any object given a box or point. We give it each footprint's box and keep its outline when it agrees with the footprint. SAMRS [22] used the same box-to-mask trick to build datasets.

labels 0.606 → 0.650, model +0.007

DINOv3 backbone

Meta's 2025 self-supervised image encoder [10], trained on 1.7 billion images without labels. Align and Segment [15] uses its ConvNeXt-Tiny version for exactly this problem (misaligned building labels).

−0.011, worse here

Edge-weighted loss

Google's Open Buildings paper [3] weights pixels near building edges up to 200× in the loss, so the model learns to separate touching buildings. 93% of our error is at edges.

no gain (−0.001)

Soft-label self-training

A trained model labels the training images again; a new model learns from those probabilities. Works when the teacher is better than the original labels, which our gate now measures directly (0.723 vs 0.650).

student = teacher+TTA in 1 pass

Test-time augmentation

Predict on the crop and its 7 rotated/flipped copies, undo the transform, average. Free at training time, +0.010 here.

kept

Label-quality gate

Before spending GPU hours, score each candidate label set against the held-out hand roofs. We found no prior paper that ranks label sources this way before training; overnight, the gate ranking and the trained ranking agreed only loosely (Spearman 0.77 single-pass, 0.54 with TTA, 6 label sets, not significant).

weak evidence so far

The energy estimate

Better segmentation barely moves the energy error bar. The single most valuable next measurement for the energy number is k_usable, the fraction of a roof that can hold panels (water tanks, stairs, shade). It is assumed at 0.60; the one verified Indian source (Bhopal) gives 0.30, which would cut capacity to about 2.9–3.3 GWp.

Three paper framings

A · Open footprints vs domain adaptation

"For Global South cities, open footprint products beat adapting Western benchmarks." Venues: CVPR EarthVision workshop, IGARSS, NeurIPS Climate Change AI workshop.

Needs: UDA-with-OB control; ideally a second city.

B · Which weak label should you train on?

Label gate + SAM + per-building alignment + soft self-training, with the gate shown to predict downstream IoU. Venues: ISPRS Geospatial Week, IGARSS; extended to IEEE JSTARS.

Needs: more label variants so the gate test has enough points (6 is too few), and a double-annotated subset.

C · Jaipur rooftop PV with quantified uncertainty

Open-label pipeline to GWp/TWh with an error budget. Venues: NeurIPS CCAI workshop, MDPI Remote Sensing, an energy journal.

Needs: measured k_usable; comparison with Google Solar API figures.

Blocker for any submission: the imagery is Google Maps tiles, whose terms forbid ML training, and the repository is public. Resolve the licence (or re-run on licensed imagery such as ISRO/NRSC or Maxar via a research agreement) before submitting anything.

Next steps

  1. Use the self-trained student st_soft_fuse08 (single pass, 0.706) for the full-city run instead of the TTA teacher: same accuracy, 8× less inference. Its checkpoint is already copied to experiments/2026-11-20-toward-higher-iou/ckpt_keep/ in the DGX home.
  2. For the label-gate claim, train 3–5 more label variants (SAM box pads, fusion weights) so the gate-vs-trained ranking can be tested as a correlation, not a top pick.
  3. Boundaries are 91% of the error: try a boundary-refinement head or a 2× upsampled input before any more label work.
  4. Measure k_usable on 100 roofs (it is 68% of the energy error).
  5. Have two annotators redraw the same 30–50 crops: turns the annotator-style finding into a result and tightens the test set.
  6. Run one UDA method with Open Buildings as its source labels (the missing control for framing A).
  7. Resolve the imagery licence.
