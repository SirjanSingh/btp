Architecture · high level

The whole system on one screen

Data flows left to right through five stages. Only stage 3 learns anything; the rest is fixed geometry and arithmetic. The hand-drawn roofs never enter training: they sit in a separate scoring loop underneath.

Method · what this is, and where each piece comes from

Weak supervision, not domain adaptation

The 0.70 model is not unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA). UDA trains on a labelled source city and adapts to an unlabelled target. We skip that: Open Buildings already gives noisy labels in Jaipur itself, so we train on the target directly and clean the labels. UDA is our comparison, and it lost.

what we use

Weakly supervised training on noisy target labels

Open Buildings footprints, shifted onto the roofs, train a U-Net directly on Jaipur. Test IoU 0.697, 0.706 with 8-way averaging.

what we tried and dropped

Unsupervised domain adaptation

Train on labelled AIRS / BDAPPV, adapt to Jaipur with AdaBN, FDA, histogram matching, self-training (arms A-R), DACS mixing, BLDA and DUDA. All scored 0.04-0.33 on the hand roofs.

tried, separate result

Fine-tune on 76 hand crops

2-fold: +0.026 one way, −0.021 the other. No reliable gain, so the zero-label model stays the headline.

Step 1 · weak supervision

Nobody labelled 9,072 crops. Google already had.

Google's Open Buildings dataset has a polygon for almost every building in Africa and South Asia, made by their own model. We draw those polygons onto our Jaipur crops and use them as the answer key for training.

They are noisy: a few buildings are missing, outlines are a bit rough, and the whole layer sits slightly off the roofs. That is why this is called weak supervision. It is not unsupervised domain adaptation: we tried those methods (AdaBN, FDA, self-training, DUDA) and they scored 0.04 to 0.33.

Step 2 · the label fix that mattered most

Slide every outline 7 px south and 3 px west

Comparing Open Buildings with our hand-drawn roofs showed the whole layer is offset by about 1.9 m: 7 pixels north and 3 east of the true roofs, in every one of the 16 tiles. So we move the label raster back before training.

Measured again today on the calibration half alone (so the test crops never influenced it): still exactly 7 south, 3 west.

Result: test IoU 0.659 → 0.697, a gain of +0.038 (95% CI +0.031 to +0.045), the same on 3 random seeds.

Step 3 · the network

U-Net with a ResNet34 encoder, layer by layer

The left half (encoder) shrinks the picture while learning what is in it. The right half (decoder) grows it back to full size to say where. The arcs are skip connections: they hand fine detail from the encoder straight to the decoder so roof edges stay sharp. 24.4 million weights, starting from ImageNet.

Input 3 × 512 × 512

A 512 × 512 RGB crop (136 m across), scaled to 0-1 and normalised with ImageNet mean and spread.

Step 4 · training

40 passes over 7,371 crops

  • Loss: half BCE (each pixel right or wrong) plus half Dice (overlap of the whole shape).
  • Optimiser: AdamW, learning rate 1e-4 for the decoder, 1e-5 for the pretrained encoder, weight decay 1e-4.
  • Schedule: cosine decay down to 1e-6; batch 16; mixed precision on a V100.
  • Augmentation: flips, 90° turns, small shifts and zooms, noise, colour jitter.
  • Model pick: the epoch with best overlap on 3 held-out tiles, scored against Open Buildings. No hand label is used.
Step 5 · from probability to roof

Pick the cut-off on one half, report on the other

The network outputs a probability for every pixel (left). A threshold turns it into roof / not roof (right). Our models are a little under-confident, so 0.4 beats the usual 0.5.

To keep the score honest, the 152 hand crops are split by tile. The threshold is chosen on the calibration tiles and the IoU is reported only on the test tiles.

Step 6 · subtract panels already there

Teach the panel detector what "no panel" looks like

The old solar models were trained only on French images that all contained panels. On Jaipur they called canals, blue tarps and metal roofs "solar".

We retrained with 15,504 French images with no panels plus 3,955 Jaipur patches of fields, roads and canals. False alarms dropped from about half to about 5%.

Existing rooftop solar in our area: 0.28 to 0.44 km² of panels, about 56 to 89 MWp. That is a lower bound, and only about 1.5% of the potential.

Step 7 · from roof area to energy

The chain of multiplications

The predicted roof area is corrected by its measured bias, multiplied by the share of roof that can actually take panels, minus panels already there, times panel efficiency, times the sunshine Jaipur gets (Global Solar Atlas). The usable share is the weakest link, so try both values.

Step 8 · how good is it

Scoreboard on real, hand-drawn roofs

IoU = overlap ÷ union of predicted and true roof pixels (1.0 is perfect). The blue line marks the best fully supervised result we found on the comparable Inria aerial benchmark (about 0.83, clean labels). Our agreed target is 0.75 to 0.80.

Round 1 · five experiments in parallel

Which change raises IoU?

Each run changes one thing against the 0.697 baseline (same seed, epochs, batch and model pick). Label variants are first scored against the hand roofs on calib and only trained if they beat the current labels.

running

Edge-weighted loss

Sirko et al. 2021: pixels near a roof edge count up to about 28× more in the cross-entropy (Gaussian σ 5.6 px). Targets the 93% of error that sits on edges.

running

DINOv3 encoder

Swap ResNet34 for a ConvNeXt-Tiny distilled from Meta's DINOv3 (31.9 M weights), the encoder Align and Segment uses.

running

Per-building shift labels

Every Open Buildings polygon moved on its own (±14 px) to best match the current model's roof prediction.

running

SAM roof outlines

Each building's box prompts SAM ViT-H; its outline replaces the footprint when the two agree.

running

Shift + SAM (hybrid labels)

SAM prompted with the per-building-shifted box: both label fixes at once.
