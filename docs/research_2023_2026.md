# What's new in segmentation (2023–2026), and what it means for this project

Researched 2026-11-20 through web search plus reading the arXiv HTML and model cards. Numbers marked
*(summary)* came from a machine summary of part of a page that could not be read word for word, so check
them against the PDF before citing. The older papers we already use are in `literature_notes.md`.

Where we stand: ResNet34 U-Net trained on Open Buildings footprints shifted by one global offset
(7 px S, 3 px W). Test IoU is 0.706 against hand-labelled roofs at 26.6 cm/px, and 93% of the error is
on roof boundaries.

---

## 1. Are we doing the right thing? Mostly yes, and 2026 papers do the same

Two 2026 papers attack exactly our problem: footprint labels that don't line up with roofs in
**non-orthorectified Google Earth imagery**.

| Paper | What it does | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **OMAF**: Xu et al., *Revisiting the Necessity of Full Accuracy: Weakly Supervised Object-Level Offset Correction for Misaligned Building Labels*, **CVPR 2026** | Estimates a **separate offset for every building**, then trains a segmenter on the corrected labels. Imagery: Google Earth Pro at 0.5 m with Microsoft footprints (Turkey). The self-alignment module adds 9.22 mIoU points (poster abstract). | Same setup as ours. Our single global shift is the simplest version of their idea. Code: github.com/dayunyan/OMAF-Building-Alignment. The licence is unclear (the README says MIT, the LICENSE file is GPL-3.0). The paper PDF was blocked (403), so the full numbers are not read. |
| **Align and Segment (AnS)**: Guthula et al., arXiv 2607.10841, **Jul 2026**, CC BY 4.0 | A small network (TNet) learns to move each label mask onto the segmenter's prediction during training, with no clean labels. It works with any segmenter. Their segmenter is a **U-Net with a ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder holding frozen DINOv3 weights**. | With a 50 px synthetic shift, IoU went 0.46 → 0.78 on Las Vegas *(summary)*. On real Google Earth (ReBO), it went 0.54 → 0.62. Code: github.com/venkanna37/align-and-segment. |
| **DragOSM**: Li et al., arXiv 2509.17951, Sep 2025 | A SAM-based model drags OSM polygons onto roofs and footprints. Releases **ReBO**: 179,265 buildings in 41 cities at 0.5 m Google Earth, with both roof **and** footprint polygons corrected by hand. | Works on polygons, so it's hard to plug into our U-Net. ReBO is useful as extra clean roof labels, once its licence is checked. |

**Takeaway for the mentor:** "train on a footprint database, then fix the misalignment" is the current
approach (CVPR 2026). It is not outdated. What we lack is (a) **per-building** offsets instead of one
global shift, and (b) a **modern pretrained encoder**.

Why per-building matters for us: Google imagery isn't orthorectified, so a building's roof moves away
from its footprint **in proportion to its height**. One global shift can't be right for both a
1-storey and a 5-storey building. Our per-crop best shifts vary by a few px (IQR in
`offset_calib.json`), which fits this.

## 2. New foundation models ("one model that segments everything")

| Model | What it is | For our roofs |
|---|---|---|
| **SAM 2 / SAM 3** (Meta; SAM 3 = "segment anything with concepts", 2025) | Give it a box, point or text and it returns a mask. SAM 3 adds text prompts ("building"). | On iSAID, SAM 3 scored mask mAP50 **57.2 with box prompts but 22.7 with text alone** *(summary, arXiv 2607.09583)*. So **text-only is weak and box prompts are strong**. We already have boxes from Open Buildings, which makes SAM a good label refiner (our step 4), not a replacement for the model. A known weakness: it merges densely packed buildings into one mask. |
| **DINOv3** (Meta, Aug 2025) | A self-supervised backbone. One version is trained on **493M Maxar satellite images at 0.6 m** (`dinov3-vitl16-pretrain-sat493m`, 300M params, gated access on HuggingFace). | Reported to beat dedicated remote-sensing foundation models on GEO-Bench, using RGB only. Downside: 16 px patches (4.3 m at our resolution) are coarse for roof edges, so it needs a U-Net-style decoder with high-res skips, or the **ConvNeXt DINOv3** variants that AnS uses. Checkpoint is about 1.2 GB (fits our quota). **This is the encoder upgrade to try.** |
| **V-JEPA 2 / 2.1, I-JEPA** (Meta) | JEPA = "joint embedding predictive architecture". It learns by predicting hidden parts of an image or video in feature space. V-JEPA 2.1 (Mar 2026) improved dense features and is competitive on segmentation linear probes. | No remote-sensing or building results found. Interesting as research, but not something we can use for roofs now. DINOv3 already gives us a strong self-supervised backbone. |
| **Vision-language models that output masks**: Gemini 2.5/3 segmentation, GeoPixel (ICML 2025, remote-sensing pixel grounding, SAM-2 based) | Ask in words, get masks back. | No published IoU on aerial buildings. Masks are coarse compared with a trained segmenter, and the zero-shot Grounded-SAM pipeline reached IoU 0.71 on WHU, where trained models get about 0.92. Fine as a demo or sanity check, **not** a path to 0.75+. |
| **Remote-sensing foundation models**: Prithvi, Clay, DOFA, TerraMind, SkySense | Mostly built for multispectral Sentinel/Landsat data at 10 m. | Wrong resolution for 26.6 cm roofs. A 2025 benchmark (GeoCrossBench) found **general-purpose DINOv3 beats them** in-distribution. Skip. |

## 3. New data we could use

- **Open Buildings 2.5D Temporal** (Google, Sep 2024): yearly 2016–2023 building presence **and height**
  for the Global South, **India included**. Built from Sentinel-2, effective resolution 4 m,
  height MAE 1.5 m. Earth Engine ID `GOOGLE/Research/open-buildings-temporal/v1`. Two uses:
  1. **Per-building offset:** a roof's displacement from its footprint scales with height. This would
     give a principled per-building shift, which is a cheap version of OMAF.
  2. **Energy:** heights let us model shading between buildings and multi-storey roofs.
- **ReBO** (with DragOSM): hand-corrected roof polygons at 0.5 m in 41 cities. A possible extra
  clean-label source for pre-training, if its licence allows.

## 4. Building-segmentation state of the art (for the "is 0.90 possible" question)

- With clean labels: WHU about 0.92 IoU (e.g. DeepSwinLite 2025), Inria about 0.83 (UANet 2024 83.1,
  UAGLNet 83.7). Inria (0.3 m, mixed cities) is the closest to our setting.
- Zero-shot foundation-model pipelines: about 0.71 on WHU (Grounded-SAM, ISPRS 2025).
- So 0.90 needs clean labels **and** an easy dataset. 0.75–0.80 remains the realistic target for us.

## 5. What to do next, in priority order

> Superseded by the revised order at the end of section 6, after measuring on our data: per-building alignment helps less than expected (+0.01 to +0.02); label shape matters more.

| # | Change | Why | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Per-building offset** of OB labels: score shifts per building against the current model's prediction (or OB 2.5D height), then retrain | Matches CVPR 2026 OMAF and AnS; aims at the boundary error | 1 day, no new data |
| 2 | **DINOv3 encoder** (ConvNeXt or sat ViT-L) in our U-Net, same labels | Best available pretrained features for satellite imagery | 1 run; about 1.2 GB download |
| 3 | **SAM 2/3 refinement** of labels with OB boxes (step 4) | Box-prompted SAM is strong; turns footprints into roof outlines | 1 day; about 2.5 GB |
| 4 | **Sirko edge-weighted loss** + Focal Tversky (γ = 0.25) | Cheap, aimed at boundaries | 1 run |
| 5 | **OB 2.5D heights** in the energy chain | Better kWh estimate (shading, floors) | Separate task |

Skip: text-only SAM 3, VLM segmentation, multispectral remote-sensing foundation models, JEPA,
and more UDA.

## 6. Code review of the two misalignment repos (2026-11-20)

### OMAF (github.com/dayunyan/OMAF-Building-Alignment, CVPR 2026)
- **How each building gets its offset** (`offset_emi.py`, `tools/alignment.py`): for each footprint, optimise (dx, dy)
  with Adam over a coarse-to-fine blur pyramid. The loss has three parts:
  1. **Edge agreement:** the moved footprint's outline should land on Canny edges of the image (distance-transform loss).
  2. **Uniform roof:** pixel colour inside the moved footprint should have low variance.
  3. **Stay close to the prior:** an L2 penalty on moving away from the starting offset.

  It runs from several starting points and keeps the best result after non-maximum suppression.
- **Confidence** (`offset_confidence.py`): the prior is a 2D Gaussian of offsets, measured on a hand-aligned
  test split. Their values are **hard-coded for Turkey**: mean (8.5, 22) px, std (8.8, 11.8) px at 0.5 m.
- **Learned correction** (`train_offset_instance.py`, `InstanceOffsetNet`): a network trained on the
  confident offsets regresses offsets for every building. `generate_corrected_labels.py` writes the corrected masks.
- **Verdict:** it's a research codebase, not a library. There are many versioned scripts (v2–v6), Chinese comments,
  a hard-coded `cuda:1` and hard-coded Gaussian values, and no weights. The licence is unclear (README says MIT, LICENSE file says GPL-3.0).
  **Reuse the idea, not the code.** The edge + uniform-roof objective is about 100 lines and needs no labels.

### Align and Segment (github.com/venkanna37/align-and-segment, MIT, 2026)
- About 2.7k lines and clean.
  - `tools/models/tnet.py`: a ViT-S predicts **one rotation + translation per training patch**, not per building.
  - `tools/training/training.py`: segmentation loss on the label after it is moved onto the prediction, plus a
    consistency loss (randomly move the label and ask TNet to undo it), 300 epochs.
- `tools/models/snet.py` is the most useful file for us. It is a U-Net whose encoder is **ConvNeXt-Tiny with DINOv3
  weights**, loaded in one line: `timm.create_model('convnext_tiny.dinov3_lvd1689m', features_only=True)`.
- **Blocker:** our DGX container has timm 0.9.2 and smp 0.3.3. AnS needs timm 1.0.28 and smp 0.5.0. We would
  install a newer timm into a folder inside the repo and put it on PYTHONPATH (no root needed).

### Measured on our own data first (`scripts/per_building_offset_check.py`)
OB masks scored against the hand roofs (pooled IoU; 2,513 buildings; results in `per_building_offset.json`):

| OB labels aligned by | calib | test |
|---|---|---|
| none (raw) | 0.520 | 0.508 |
| one global shift (current) | 0.551 | 0.535 |
| per building, moved onto our model's prediction (label-free, AnS-style) | 0.567 | 0.549 |
| per building, moved onto the hand mask (**ceiling**; uses test labels, so not usable for training) | 0.609 | 0.584 |

What this shows:
- **Per-building alignment can only improve label quality by about 0.05 IoU, even with perfect alignment.** The
  label-free version gets about 0.014 of that. For scale, the global shift added 0.027 to label IoU, and that
  became +0.038 model IoU.
- So per-building alignment is worth roughly **+0.01 to +0.02** model IoU. It is real, but small.
- **The bigger problem is shape, not position.** Even perfectly placed, OB footprints only reach 0.58 IoU
  against the hand roofs, and our model already scores 0.706. The footprints have the wrong outline:
  footprint instead of roof, simplified polygons, merged or missing buildings. Moving them can't fix that.
- Per-building offsets do vary a lot (std about 6–8 px), but the median is exactly our global (7, −3). Small and
  large buildings have the same median offset, so there is no height parallax visible at this scale.

### Revised priorities after reading the code and measuring
1. **Fix label shape:** SAM box-prompted refinement of OB footprints (step 4). This targets the 0.58 ceiling directly.
2. **DINOv3 ConvNeXt encoder** (copy AnS `snet.py`, upgrade timm into a local folder). One run.
3. **Per-building alignment to the model prediction:** cheap (the script exists), expected +0.01 to +0.02.
   Run it as a label variant together with 1.
4. Sirko edge-weighted loss.
